RJ Inventory & Sales — User Guide

For the store owner. Open the app by double-clicking index.html in the app folder. You need an internet connection to save anything.

1. Getting started

  • Sign in with your username and password. Forgot it? Use Forgot password on the sign-in screen; a reset link goes to your recovery email.
  • The menu on the left groups the screens: Overview, Sales, Stock, Finance and System. On a small screen, open it with the ☰ button.
  • After some minutes without use the screen locks; enter your password to continue. Change the time in Settings → General.
  • If the internet drops, a banner says Offline. You can still look at what was already loaded, but nothing can be saved until the connection is back.

2. First-time setup of your records

Do these once, in this order, when you start using the system:

  1. Settings → General: business name, address and contact number (they appear on receipts and statements).
  2. Settings → Sales: Sale ID prefix (default RJ-), default number of installments (default 2), due-date pattern and reminder message.
  3. Items: add each rice item with its price, cost and opening stock (the quantity you have today). Link each per-kg item to its sack item so you can open sacks.
  4. Customers: add your regular customers with a contact number.
  5. Opening balances (System menu): for every customer who still owes from your Excel records, add an opening balance — original date, a short description, and what is still to be paid (one amount, or 2–5 installments with due dates; Split evenly fills the amounts and monthly dates for you). Opening balances are collected like sales but are not counted as sales, revenue or profit, and they do not change stock. You can also start one from the customer's page (Opening balance button).
  6. Backup: download your first backup (see Backup).
Want to try things first? Settings → System → Load sample data (asks for your password) fills the system with example items, customers, sales, opening balances and more. In the real database these sample records stay, so use it only on a test setup.

3. Recording a sale

  1. Click New sale. Choose the customer (or Walk-in for a paid counter sale; type part of the name or phone number to search).
  2. Add items and quantities. The screen shows stock on hand and stops you from selling more than you have. Stock is taken from the oldest batch first; the line shows which batches it uses. The price comes from those batches: when a line takes from two batches with different prices, it uses the average (for example 2 sacks at ₱1,500 and 3 at ₱1,550 → ₱1,530 each). You may change the unit price on a line; the batch or credit price stays visible (pay-later terms use the item's credit price) with use it to go back.
  3. To sell from a particular batch (for example a damaged sack), choose it in the Batch list under the line.
  4. Optional discount in % or ₱ (on the items only).
  5. Optional Other charges: click Add charge and type a name and amount, for example Delivery fee ₱50. Names you used before are suggested with their last amount. Charges are added after the discount and are part of any balance or installments.
  6. Choose the terms: Paid in full, Partial / pay later (with an optional due date) or Installment.
  7. Choose how they paid (Cash, Bank transfer, E-wallet; add the reference number for transfers).
  8. Set the delivery status if you deliver, then Save sale. Print the receipt from the sale's page.

Saved sales cannot be edited (only delivery status, delivery date and notes). To correct a mistake, Cancel sale with a reason: stock goes back and any payments are recorded as refunded. Then enter it again.

4. Installments and balances

  • An installment plan has 2, 3, 4 or 5 payments (2 is the default). Choose the due-date pattern: every 15th, every end of month, or twice a month (15th and end of month). A down payment is optional.
  • Amounts are whole pesos; the last installment takes any remainder, so the total always matches. You can change any due date before saving.
  • Later, Reschedule on the sale page changes due dates and amounts of unpaid installments (the total must stay the same). Every change is kept in the history.
  • An installment is overdue when its due date has passed and it is not fully paid.

5. Payments and collections

  • Record payment on the sale page, the customer page or the Collections screen. The amount goes to the oldest unpaid installment first. If the customer pays more than the balance, the system tells you how much change to give.
  • Partial payment and promised date: if a payment leaves part of an installment that is due (or overdue) unpaid, enter the date the customer promised to pay the rest. The promise is added on top: the installment's due date and the next installments stay as they are. It appears on the Calendar as its own entry (so a promise on the same day as the next due date shows two entries), in Collections → Promised, in the sale's schedule and on the customer statement. A promise is kept when the rest is paid by that date (it then counts as paid on time), missed otherwise — the installment stays overdue from its original due date and you can add a new promise with Add promise in the sale's schedule.
  • A wrong payment is voided with a reason (never deleted). It stays visible, crossed out. A promise made with that payment is removed with it.
  • Collections lists everything still owed: Overdue first, then Due today, Next 7 days, Later, No due date and Promised. Use Copy reminder to paste a friendly message into SMS or Messenger.

6. Customers

Each customer page shows the balance, credit limit, how reliably they pay, alternate contacts, and tabs for Sales, Payments, Schedule and a printable Statement of account. A customer who still owes cannot be archived. The built-in Walk-in customer is for fully paid counter sales only.

7. Items and stock

  • SKU: every item gets a code like RJ-DIN-001 — RJ, the first 3 letters of the variety (or of the name when there is no variety) and a number. Leave the SKU blank when adding an item and the app gives one; you can type your own (it must be unique). The SKU is shown wherever items are picked or listed, on receipts and reports, and you can search by it.
  • Each item has a cash price and a credit price (pay later / installments; empty = same as cash). A new sale on Open balance or Installment fills in the credit price; you can still change it on the line.
  • Every stock change is recorded as a movement (Stock → Movements): opening stock, sales, cancellations, restocks, adjustments, counts and opened sacks.
  • Batches: each restock delivery becomes a batch with its own number (for example RJ-RS-SUP001-0004: supplier SUP001, its 4th delivery), cost and cash price. The item page lists the batches in stock, oldest first; the oldest is sold first. Use Edit on a batch to change its cost or cash price (later sales only). Stock you had before batches existed was put into a first batch (RJ-RS-OLD-…) at the item's cost and price.
  • The Cash price shown for an item is the price of its oldest batch; next shows the next batch's price when it differs. Changing the item's cash price asks whether to apply it to the batches in stock too.
  • Adjust stock for spillage, spoilage and similar, with a reason (removed from the oldest batch unless you choose one).
  • Open sack moves one or more sacks into the linked per-kg item. The kilos keep the sack's batch number; their cost is the sack batch cost ÷ sack weight unless you type one.
  • Stock count: start a count, enter what you counted, then finalize; the differences become adjustments.
  • Items below their minimum stock are flagged on the Items list and the Dashboard.

8. Restocks, suppliers and expenses

  • Create a restock order for a supplier. Stock increases only when you Receive it (all or part). On receiving, enter for each item the quantity received, the unit cost, its own expense (total for that line, e.g. unloading; optional), the cash price and the credit price; the delivery becomes one batch. A changed credit price becomes the item's credit price. Tick Make these the item's default cost and price if new batches should start from these values.
  • New item on the restock order form opens the same form as Items → Add item, so you can order an item you have never sold without leaving the restock.
  • Restock expenses: an item's own Expense stays on that item; below the items, add the delivery's other expenses (Trucking, Fuel, Labor … type any name; names used before are suggested). The total is split over the items by value, and each item shows its cost incl. expenses per unit and the profit per unit at the cash price you type (example: 150 sacks at ₱2,500 with ₱6,000 expenses → ₱40 per sack → ₱2,540). This only helps you set the price; the batch cost stays ₱2,500. Each expense (the items' own ones too) is saved on the Expenses page as category Restock, linked to the restock.
  • Edit a delivery: on the restock page, Edit next to a delivery changes its unit cost, expense, cash price, credit price and other expenses, even after the order is complete. A new cost also changes the profit of sales already made from that batch. Quantities cannot be changed there (use a stock adjustment).
  • Every supplier has a code (SUP001, SUP002 …) used in its batch numbers.
  • Cancel an order if nothing arrived; Close it if the rest will not come.
  • Expenses (rent, wages, fuel …) reduce net profit. Wrong entries are voided, not deleted. Restock expenses are changed on their restock, not on the Expenses page.

9. Calendar

Shows sales, payment due dates, overdue installments, promised payment dates, deliveries and expected restocks, plus your own reminders. Use the filters at the top. Click an entry to see its details in a pop-up (customer, amounts, items, dates); use Open sale or Open restock there only if you want the full record. Your own reminders have an Edit button in the pop-up.

10. Dashboard and reports

  • The Dashboard shows today's sales, this month's revenue, profit and cash collected, receivables, overdue amounts, stock value and what needs attention.
  • Reports: Sales, Profit by item, Profit & loss, Cash collected, Receivables aging, Inventory valuation, Movements & losses, Restocks by supplier. Pick a period, then Print / PDF or Export CSV (opens in Excel).
  • Revenue counts sales on their sale date; cash collected counts payments on the day received. Opening balances count toward receivables and cash collected, never toward revenue.
  • Profit uses the real cost of the batches each sale took. Other charges (delivery fee …) are part of revenue and shown on their own line in Profit & loss; Profit by item leaves them out.

11. Backup and restore

Download a backup at least once a week. The Dashboard reminds you when the last one is older than 7 days.

  1. System → Backup → Download backup. A file such as rj-bugasan-backup-2026-11-25-1430.json is saved to your Downloads folder.
  2. Copy it somewhere safe: a USB drive, or email it to yourself. Keep several.
Restore replaces all current records with the ones in the file; anything entered after the backup is lost. Use it only to recover from a serious mistake or after moving to a new database. Choose the file, check the record counts, then type RESTORE to confirm. If the internet drops during a restore, the Backup screen says so: choose the same file and restore again. User accounts are never changed by a restore.

12. Activity log

System → Activity log lists every important action — sales, payments, voids, price and cost changes, stock adjustments, restores — with who did it and when. Search by text, record number or person, filter by area and dates, and click a record number to open it. Entries cannot be changed or deleted.

13. Settings and security

  • General: business details, idle-lock minutes. Sales: numbering and installment defaults. Appearance: light/dark theme, density (spacing) and sidebar labels. Account: your sign-in details. System: version, database connection, sample data.
  • Change your password from the menu under your name (top right). Use a strong password and do not share it.
  • The app folder can be copied to another PC; everyone signs in to the same online database.

14. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
"You do not have permission…"The database rules are not published or are out of date. Ask your administrator to publish firestore.rules (see the go-live checklist).
"The database needs an index…"Publish firestore.indexes.json; new indexes take a few minutes to build.
Offline banner, cannot saveCheck the internet connection. Saving resumes automatically once you are back online.
Locked screenEnter your password. This happens after the idle time set in Settings → General.
Numbers look different on another PCWait a moment — the screens update live. Refresh the page if it stays out of date.

Version and connection details: Settings → System.
